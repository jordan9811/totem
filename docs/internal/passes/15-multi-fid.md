## What it found

- The three adversarial reviewers put the same three findings first: a live account for a SEEDED persona would be owned by a custody key anyone can derive from the repository, the plain persona to fid map was one signed document listing every account of a root, and the anon "window" was one-per-epoch because the qualifier had been appended beside the epoch instead of replacing it. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > 'GATE 5 — Linkage test v4 + three adversarial reviews + posture "Network identities"'}
- The pass's own safety-set suite caught the map finding one gate before the reviewers did: once a fork minted a fid for the new persona, Gate 1's plain map put that persona's id on the validator as a plaintext address, and the finding was recorded as open rather than quietly carried. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 4 — Anon-post mode (the anoncast pattern, inside Layers)"}
- A product defect surfaced only once a test sent the right cookie: the classic timeline resolved a cast's persona marker through the validator alone, and the validator never learns a persona this client MINTED, so a minted persona's reply rendered unresolved with the marker leaking into the text. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 2 — Live: two real persona accounts"}
- One defect behind five failing checks: the validator's scope-string builder fell through the new anon branch into the attestation branch, which rejects a qualifier, so every anon envelope was refused and the checks downstream had nothing to work with. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 4 — Anon-post mode (the anoncast pattern, inside Layers)"}
- A crashed device-loss check left a new blank root on disk whose device-auth anchor the validator refused first-write-wins, with the old root's sealed namespaces unreadable — the earlier pass's anchor contract doing its job, at the cost of a rig repair. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 1 — Persona ↔ FID model + signer custody"}
- Running two harnesses at once corrupted a run's last two suites with unreachable-server failures, and the rule for the rest of the pass became one harness at a time, launched only after the previous one exits. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 3 — Fork → new FID, surface regression in both modes"}
- The reveal endpoint took the attributing fid on the caller's word, so someone holding a post's secret could have it recorded against another person's fid; our client would not have rendered it, and unrenderable is not refused. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "ADDITION 2 — the reveal is signed (posture §14.2)"}
- The closing sweeps found four more defects across four series, none of them security holes: a fork naming rule too strict for the product's own recovery flow, a ledger write outside its pinned module, a seed reset that missed one table, and a suite's own retry gap. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "Closing series — five attempts, one real code fix each time the sweeps found something"}

## Judgment calls

- Creating a real Farcaster account is the USER's action: the pass ships the capped, dry-run-first, one-command path and the app seals the receipt, but the app never creates an account and never spends by itself. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 1 — Persona ↔ FID model + signer custody"}
- A live account only for a persona whose Farcaster keys come off the root phrase; a seeded persona's keys are public by construction and are refused before any billable call, correcting the earlier plan's "two seeded personas". {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > 'GATE 5 — Linkage test v4 + three adversarial reviews + posture "Network identities"'}
- No plain persona to fid map on the validator at all, reversing Gate 1's decision: nothing in the product read it, and the relation resolves from the sealed record. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > 'GATE 5 — Linkage test v4 + three adversarial reviews + posture "Network identities"'}
- The window index REPLACES the epoch in the scope string on both sides, and the validator refuses the grace epoch for anon posts, so the rule is one nullifier per root per window rather than per epoch. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > 'GATE 5 — Linkage test v4 + three adversarial reviews + posture "Network identities"'}
- The predicate an anon post proves is the tree the proof lives in, and the VALIDATOR enforces its own configured copy: the relay's predicate is the relay's rule, never the client's claim. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 4 — Anon-post mode (the anoncast pattern, inside Layers)"}
- The composer's anon toggle never posts: the text waits in the gate's wanted set, the next tick proves it, and the drain sends it at a jittered time, so the composer says "queued" and the post appears when relayed. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 4 — Anon-post mode (the anoncast pattern, inside Layers)"}
- A weak fact is not signed at all: the region issuer refuses when the location carries a VPN caveat, and the age issuer supports no bucket for an account that ever changed its username, because "zero username changes" is part of the claim rather than a footnote on an older account. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "GATE 0 — Housekeeping + onboarding issuers"}
- Possession of the secret is the authority for a reveal, nothing reveals automatically or on anyone else's schedule, and the first reveal wins permanently — a reveal is public and irreversible by design, which is what makes it worth anything to a reader. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "ADDITION (after the pass closed) — anon-post ATTRIBUTION: the anoncast reveal pattern"}
- Rendering "this persona posted it" is the READER's assertion, so the reader ties the verified key to the fid; with no answer the cast stays plain, and the old fallback that rendered a bare fid was deleted because "we could not check this" must never look like a weaker version of the claim. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "ADDITION 2 — the reveal is signed (posture §14.2)"}
- The validator carries evidence and is not an authority on identity: it verifies and stores the key a reveal was signed under, and deliberately holds no binding from a fid to a key. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "ADDITION 2 — the reveal is signed (posture §14.2)"}

## Verification result

- Bar met on the closing commit: four consecutive clean sweeps, 478 of 478 checks over 64 suites, zero infra, four times. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "Series 5 — `526addf` (the closing series)"}
- Getting there took five series; every series but the last halted on its first sweep at a genuine finding, never patched blind and never restarted on a guess. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "Closing series — five attempts, one real code fix each time the sweeps found something"}
- The runner refuses to launch a suite unless both processes answer the signed health contract and the app's peer is the validator under test. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "/verify — the closing bar"}
- Restart survival and total-device-loss recovery ran inside every sweep, the recovery bringing a minted persona's signer key, custody address and account records back bit for bit from the 24-word phrase. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "/verify — the closing bar"}
- The greps ran every iteration over the whole database, the process log and the envelope on the wire, and found no credential, key, address or persona id beside any cast in all four closing sweeps. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "/verify — the closing bar"}
- Total live spend for the pass: zero, with no live artifacts on disk. {cite: notes/reports/REPORT-FID.md > "/verify — the closing bar"}
